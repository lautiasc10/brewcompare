Our take

What it is actually like

The Anima Prestige gets several things right that matter. At 22 cm wide it is the narrowest machine in this catalogue, which is the whole argument for it if your counter is tight. The ceramic flat burr grinder runs cool. The brew group pulls out and is rated for around 20,000 cycles. From cold it delivers a first cup in about 45 seconds. Set to the finest grind, maximum dose and highest temperature, it makes an espresso that holds its own against anything else near this price.

Then there is the sensor. Coffeedant's review documents a widely reported bean-detection fault, estimated to affect somewhere between 40% and 60% of units in the first year: the machine falsely reports that it has no beans and wastes the grind. The fixes are a warranty repair with a long turnaround, or an electrical modification that voids the warranty. That is not a footnote on a $700–850 machine, and it is the single reason this one sits where it does in our value score.

The rest of the compromises are ordinary for the price. Five grind settings is coarse — the Magnifica Evo gives you thirteen. The single thermoblock means the machine brews, then steams, then cools down, so a second milk drink takes about ninety seconds longer. Milk lands at 125–135 °F, which several reviewers call cooler than they want. And the interface is a blue text display with no profiles and no app.

What works

  • Narrowest machine here at 22 cm — fits counters others do not
  • Ceramic flat burr grinder that runs cool
  • First cup from cold in about 45 seconds
  • Removable brew group rated for around 20,000 cycles
  • Milk foam density approaching café standard

What does not

  • Widely reported bean-sensor fault, estimated at 40–60% of units in year one
  • Only 5 grind settings — the coarsest adjustment in this catalogue
  • Single thermoblock: a second milk drink takes about 90 seconds longer
  • Milk output at 125–135 °F is cooler than many people want
  • Dated blue text display, no user profiles
  • 1-year warranty as standard, the shortest here

Ideal for

Someone with a genuinely narrow counter who wants one-touch milk drinks from medium roasts, and who is comfortable dealing with a warranty claim if the sensor fault appears.

Probably not for

Anyone who wants a machine to just work, or who drinks light roasts — five grind steps will not get you there.
