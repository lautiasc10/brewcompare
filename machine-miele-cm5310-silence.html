Our take

What it is actually like

Noise is the specification nobody publishes and everybody notices at seven in the morning. The grinder on a superautomatic runs for ten to fifteen seconds per drink at somewhere between 65 and 79 dB, and in an open-plan flat that is the difference between coffee and an argument. Miele built the CM 5310 around solving exactly that, called it Silence, and reviewers consistently confirm it is the quietest machine in its class. Miele does not publish a decibel figure, so the table here shows a dash — but this is the one machine in the catalogue where the marketing name is doing honest work.

What surprises people is that the coffee is not an afterthought. Reviewers describe the espresso as full-bodied and syrupy with excellent crema, from a stainless steel conical burr grinder and a genuinely well-built brew path. The materials are plastic with stainless steel parts, and the assembly quality is a clear step above the De'Longhi and Philips machines at similar money. OneTouch for Two brews a pair of any drink at once.

The interface is where Miele decided to save. It is a text display with buttons — no colour, no touchscreen, no user profiles beyond a memo function; for those you step up to the CM 5510. Five grind settings is coarse, the same limitation the Gaggia has. The 1.3-litre tank and 200-gram hopper are small. None of that stops it making excellent coffee quietly; all of it explains why it scores below machines that cost half as much on ease of use.

  • Removable brew unit and dishwasher-safe contact parts
  • Rated 4.6/5 across more than 600 verified reviews

What does not

  • Text display with buttons — the most dated interface in this catalogue
  • No user profiles, only a memo function
  • Only 5 grind settings
  • Small 1.3 L tank and 200 g hopper
  • Miele publishes no decibel figure, so 'quietest' rests on reviewer consensus
  • No water filter system listed

Ideal for

Anyone whose kitchen shares a wall with a bedroom, who values a well-made quiet machine and excellent espresso over a colour screen and a long drinks menu.

Probably not for

A household that wants per-person profiles, a big menu, or a modern touchscreen — you can have all three for less elsewhere.
