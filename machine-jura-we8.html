Our take

What it is actually like

Everything that distinguishes the WE8 is about how long it runs unattended. The tank holds three litres, nearly double the biggest domestic machine in this catalogue. The hopper takes 500 grams of beans. The grounds container holds about 25 servings. Jura rates it for roughly 30 specialty cups a day. In a home those numbers are pointless; in an office of eight to twelve people they are the difference between a machine that works and a machine somebody has to babysit.

The coffee side is solid Jura. The Aroma G3 grinder doses 5 to 16 grams with active bean-level monitoring, there are eight strength levels and two brewing temperatures, and the Fine Foam frother makes the milk drinks that keep an office happy. The CLEARYL Smart filter uses RFID so the machine knows which filter is fitted and when it was changed — a small thing that matters enormously in a shared kitchen, where nobody remembers.

What it is not is good value in a house. At list price it costs more than the Jura E8, which makes a better cup thanks to Pulse Extraction, and it gives you a button-driven TFT rather than a touchscreen. The warranty is one year, the shortest here alongside the Gaggia. And like every Jura, the brew unit is sealed. Buy it because you need the capacity and the unattended running time. If you do not, almost anything else here is a better purchase.

What works

  • 3 L water tank and 500 g bean hopper — by far the largest capacity here
  • 25-serving grounds container, so it runs a full office day untouched
  • Aroma G3 grinder with 5–16 g dosing and active bean-level monitoring
  • 8 coffee strength levels and 2 brewing temperatures
  • CLEARYL Smart filter with RFID so the machine tracks its own maintenance
  • Adjustable spouts from 2.5 to 6 inches for any cup or glass

What does not

  • Costs more than the Jura E8, which makes a better cup
  • No Pulse Extraction Process at this price
  • 1-year warranty — the shortest in this catalogue
  • Sealed brew unit, and Jura consumables for the life of the machine
  • Button-driven display rather than a touchscreen
  • The widest machine here at 29.5 cm, and the tallest at 41.9 cm

Ideal for

An office of roughly eight to twelve people, or anywhere a machine has to keep going all day without someone refilling it between drinks.

Probably not for

A home. You would pay a premium for capacity you never use, and get a worse cup than the cheaper E8.
