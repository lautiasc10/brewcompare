Our take

What it is actually like

The Magnifica Evo is the machine most people should start with, and that is not faint praise. De'Longhi put a genuine steel conical burr grinder with 13 settings inside a machine that regularly sells under six hundred dollars, and the extraction hardware behind it is the same 15-bar thermoblock arrangement used further up the range. From cold, the first cup lands in around 38 seconds, which is fast enough that you never find yourself waiting for it in the morning.

Where it saves money is the interface and the milk. The control panel is a backlit touch strip rather than a colour screen, and there are no user profiles, so a two-person household shares one set of settings. Milk drinks go through a traditional steam wand. That is genuinely worse for convenience than a one-touch carafe, and it is the single biggest reason to spend more elsewhere. It is also, if you care about it, the only system here that lets you texture milk yourself and actually improve with practice.

Maintenance is a strong point. The brew group pulls out and rinses under the tap, most contact parts are dishwasher safe, and the machine runs an automatic rinse when you switch it on and off. The one consistent complaint in owner reviews is noise: reviewers measure the grinder at 65–70 dB for the ten to fifteen seconds it runs, which is loud enough to notice in an open-plan kitchen early in the morning.

What works

  • Real steel conical burr grinder with 13 settings at an entry price
  • First cup from cold in about 38 seconds
  • Removable brew group rinses under the tap in seconds
  • Dedicated iced coffee mode that brews stronger so it does not dilute
  • Cheapest running cost of the three machines here

What does not

  • Milk is a manual steam wand — no one-touch cappuccino
  • Grinder measured at 65–70 dB, the noise owners mention most
  • Plastic housing feels its price next to a Jura
  • Single user profile, so shared households overwrite each other's settings

Ideal for

Someone moving up from pods or drip who wants freshly ground espresso at the lowest sensible price, and does not mind learning to steam milk by hand.

Probably not for

Anyone who wants to press one button and be handed a cappuccino.
